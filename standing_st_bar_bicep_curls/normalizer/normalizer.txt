BICEP CURL NORMALIZER: GEOMETRY STRATEGY
# Version: 1.0 (Sagittal Spine Alignment)
================================================================

1. THE CORE OBJECTIVE: "THE VIRTUAL PLUMB LINE"
----------------------------------------------
In a profile-view Bicep Curl, the most common error is "Back Lean." 
However, if the camera is tilted or the user is standing on a 
slight incline, a 0-degree X-coordinate check will fail. 

The Normalizer creates a "Virtual Plumb Line" by aligning the user's 
Hip (24) and Shoulder (12) to a perfectly vertical axis.

2. REFINED ROTATION LOGIC
-------------------------

A. The Hip-Centric Pivot (Stability Anchor)
- STRATEGY: Explicitly set the pivot of rotation to the Hip (24).
- RATIONALE: During a curl, the lower body should remain a 
  stationary pillar. By pivoting at the hip, we can accurately 
  measure how much the upper body "sways" away from this anchor.



B. Sagittal Plane Rectification (X-Y Correction)
- CALCULATION: We measure the slope of the spine (Hip to Shoulder).
- ACTION: Apply a rotation matrix to the X-Y plane to force the 
  spine vector to -90°.
- BENEFIT: This normalizes the "Starting Posture." Any subsequent 
  movement of the shoulder backward is now a "True Lean" relative 
  to the starting position, not a camera artifact.

3. DATA INTEGRITY & DEPTH PRESERVATION
--------------------------------------
- CONSTRAINT: Rotation is performed on the X-Y plane (the 2D side view).
- Z-AXIS: The Z-depth remains raw to monitor "Elbow Flare" (the 
  elbow moving away from the body towards or away from the camera).

4. TEMPORAL STABILIZATION
-------------------------
- METHOD: EMA Smoothing (Alpha 0.25).
- IMPACT: This stabilizes the "Elbow Anchor." Without this, a 
  shaky camera would make it look like the user is "swinging" 
  their elbows when they are actually stationary.



NOTE: This normalization step is critical for catching "Cheat Reps." 
By forcing the spine to be vertical at the start of the process, 
the Rep Analyzer can use a simple degree threshold (e.g., >7° lean) 
to flag bad form.
